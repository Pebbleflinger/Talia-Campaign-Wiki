An island off the coast of [[Quadira]], south of [[Taldor]]. Ildilas is frequently considered to be part of Quadira, though the Ildic don't share the sentiment. [[Naami Jamas|Naami]] is from Ildilas.

Most of Ildilas is a desert, and its population lives in small clusters at the edges of the island, where sheer mountains provide shade from the burning sun. These mountains form a near-perfect ring around the island, with a break to the north. There, the mountains give way to the greenery of Bottleneck Coast. The largest settlement of Ildilas, also called Bottleneck, sits on this coast.

Ildilas is populated mostly by [[Halfling]]s and [[Human]]s. The Ildic language is a dialect of the Halfling language; it is a rhythmic and archaic language that places great emphasis on syllable count. Though the Halflings of Ildilas are content to stay in their homes and make the best out of the harsh climate, the Humans sometimes venture inland to explore the desert.

In recent times, Ildilas has been inaccessible by any means of transport, magical or otherwise. A storm surrounds the island, and even attempts to teleport there or contact someone on the island by means of a Sending spell fail. After researching the unnatural weather, and speaking of Ildic legends with [[Inas]], Naami concluded the storm is made by a flight of blue dragons... maybe even [[Ishtar]] herself.

Naami's research also turned up the origin of the island itself; Ildilas is the summit of a mountain that once dominated the Sea Elven Empire, now sunken beneath the waves. The Ildic have stories of finding tower-tops buried in the desert.

The Ildic have a unique tradition of sorcery, which they call witchcraft. Though they also have many priests of [[Sarenrae]] and [[Pharasma]], many Ildic spellcasters choose to become witches who tend to focus on healing magic (though they often have other powers). Unlike other forms of sorcery, the source of their powers is not within themselves. Their bloodline carries instead an unusual conductivity to magic. While this conductivity makes spellcasting easier, it puts constant stress on the caster's body. Ildic witches wear [[Spellward|spellwards]] to inhibit their own ability for magic, a practice which has become central to their culture and traditions.

On Ildilas, a child who chooses to become a witch goes through several trials of increasing difficulty. The final tests are finding their way home through the wilderness, and being without their spellwards for an hour.

## Geography

Ildilas is a mountain top. Its cliffs rise sheer out of the ocean, not overlooking the water but leaning slightly away from it. The rock is a sandy red in colour. On the land side, the cliffs similarly lean away instead of overhanging. From the sky, the island looks like a deep crater about 60 miles in diameter.

While the centre of the island is a desert, most of its outskirts are also arid places. Villages are sometimes carved into the mountains, going up instead of digging down. Vegetation is scarce and dry, and people make the most out of what water comes down the cliffs. 

The exception to this harsh climate is Bottleneck Coast, where people tend acacia groves and strictly manage irrigation to keep the land green. The heat is still intense, and winds coming from the desert regularly damage crops and sometimes even structures.

## Society

Contrary to popular belief, the Halflings and Humans who call themselves Ildic are not native to the island. The reasons for settling on Ildilas are long-forgotten, but they consider themselves of this land now. The Halflings focus on sustainability, and carve comfort out of survival. They tend the land, raise livestock, and see to institutions. They seldom leave the settlement they were born in, and rarely leave the island. The Humans gravitate more towards trade between settlements, making and selling artisan goods, and hunting. When people lead expeditions into the desert, they are almost always led by Humans.

## Faith

The Ildic are a religious people. They believe most strongly in two celestial deities representing the sun and the moon. Called Laheya and Sareta in Ildic, these twin goddesses are seen more as aspects of life, death, and magic than clearly defined individuals. Other deities are worshipped, but they are considered to be favoured by one of the two goddesses. Temples and shrines are usually dedicated to either Sareta or Laheya, and great care is taken to ensure both deities have the same amount of public places of worship. Individual homes will usually have two small shrines side-by-side.

Laheya is thought to preside over the aspect of life, light, and fire. She is depicted as a joyful woman with red hair, smiling and dancing. Her priests encourage positivity and passion. She also represents impulse and destruction. Fires and sandstorms are understood to be her doing.

Sareta represents death, darkness, and cold. She is depicted as a serene woman with black hair, head bowed in contemplation. Her priests encourage meditation and reflection. Though she is a deity of death, she also represents recovery from injury and illness. She blesses the hot landscape with cold night, and creates fear in the heart of people to protect them from danger.

Though fear is understood to be a blessing from Sareta, it is widely used as a tool for teaching. Children are instructed to fear the dangers of the world, to fear magic, and to fear themselves. This will keep them safe. Children who train in magic are especially instructed to fear their own potential and always keep a part of their minds cool with fright.

Likely a result of their dual faith, the Ildic's spells tend to lean more towards one aspect of divinity; light or dark. Even if people cast the exact same spell, it can manifest very differently. For instance, a healing spell can appear as a flame scouring away the wound to a scar, or as shadow binding the wound before fading. It is not unheard of for a spellcaster to manifest both light and dark magic, but an equal proportion of both is exceedingly rare.

## Divination and Witchcraft

Due to their harsh environment, the Ildic have focused on healing and peering into the future. When a child is born, when a decision must be made, when new crops are planted, diviners read the omens or commune with the land or the goddesses. Many priests favour divination magic to reassure and guide the faithful.

Witchcraft (or sorcery) is less tied to religion and is seen more as magic born of the land and the people's connection to it. People who choose to become witches are often conduits for magic and are capable of strong magic, though it comes with a higher cost. The term "witch" is used for a spellcaster who is mainly a healer, regardless of their gender.

## An additional note on spellwards

Almost all children in Ildilas are given spellwards from infancy. They are usually a thin ankle chain that can't be taken off. Children are taught about magic and its dangers from an early age, and when they reach age nine they are tested for their aptitude for magic. Those who have little to no aptitude for magic tend to wear spellwards out of habit or tradition. Those who do have the potential for magic, if they choose to pursue it, go through the rituals to become a priest or a witch.

Spellwards are made of an alloy that is silvery in colour. They can take almost any form of jewellery. Bracelets and necklaces are popular, but ankle chains, rings, and earrings are also commonplace. Only people who have passed all their trials as a priest or a witch earn the right to wear spellwards that don't have a clasp and can be taken off quickly.
